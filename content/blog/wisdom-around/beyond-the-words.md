Oftentimes when we want to express our feelings with words, we notice a dissociation between our words and the actual feelings we want to convey. Yet, look at the great movies, songs, poems, and books through which we have connected to those characters, lyrics, or events that convey those feelings which makes this art so special to us. All these pieces articulate feelings far deeper than our own vocabulary allows us to grasp. The writers behind these scenes possess excellent skills for bridging words and emotions. Does this mean we lack communication skills? Perhaps, but it is often more complex than that. Many times, people are too dissociated and distanced from their feelings—they might not even be able to access them, let alone convey them.

Even children suffer from this problem. A student practitioner therapist once told me that, in order to allow kids to express their feelings, they utilize animated characters and objects, such as animals and cartoons. We start to feel from the day we're born, but speaking is different. Communication is a skill one needs to cultivate; words and language are proxies for feelings—they are not feelings themselves.

## Why it is necessary to express?

Everything we do is an expression of ourselves. For example, when we're in the bad mood or stressed we'd noticed the decline in the quality of our work. While if we're happy, quality of work gets improved. Knowingly or unknowingly our emotions slip into every other activity. 

Somewhere in life, we learn that certain feelings aren't valid. But we all need to remember that we have a deep yearning to understand ourselves and be understood. We can't force others to understand us as we want them to, but when we understand ourselves better, we learn to be fine with being misunderstood. This allows us to feel psychologically safe and secure.

## Where to go, if word fails us?

Whenever I played sports, I felt happier/more content than watching them; the same goes for writing over reading, or performing improv over watching a stand-up act. Basically, this **process of creation in itself is beautiful**.

These are all **expressions of ourselves meant to connect with ourselves deeply**. Here, we perform any of those acts with internal motivation, and our purpose compass ensures that our actions and words are genuine expressions of who we are, rather than reactions to how others perceive us.

You probably know that meditation and yoga work wonders for the health, but so do dancing, acting, painting, or even the simple rhythm of getting ready in the morning or taking a walk. **Any creation, expression, activity that makes you care about yourself.**

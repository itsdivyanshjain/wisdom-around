Two years ago, I found myself in the comfort of a remote job. However, I was in my early 20s, and even though **I knew what I wanted from life, I was not confident about whether that was truly what I wanted**. The only way I could think of to gain clarity was by gaining diverse experience, trying new things, and actively pushing myself out of the routine safety net of home and established privilege. That's when I decided to move out. I chose Bangalore because I was slightly familiar with the city due to a prior internship experience. I planned to find a new job and go for activities like security events, hacker & builder rooms, and explore hobbies like reading, sports, and improv.

## Init

Bangalore was a really fun experience for me. Here, I met ambitious people, went out more on the weekends, and enjoyed the pleasant weather. I started to experience great peaks of high and low emotions, which I was not accustomed to. There were days when I was overwhelmed by sheer joy to the point of sleeplessness.

However, this also came with internal crises, utter dissatisfaction, loneliness, and anxiety. Even though I really enjoyed my work, and it brought great satisfaction from building new ideas, hacking, and learning, I am now feeling restless and close to burnout because I am driving myself out of fear and constantly seeking the next goal. **I'm utilizing my work as an escape**. Along with this, there is uncertainty about the future because I no longer know what to do. I tried a few things that I'd planned, but they resulted in a dead end.

So, I figured out ways to calm myself down and then focused on one simple thing: getting a new job and working on one small project because I was genuinely curious about it. At this moment, I am no longer upskilling myself; I am just being patient because I do not have the capacity to learn new things. After a few months, I got the job offer, and it's in a new city.

In hindsight, I really enjoyed the city, though it technically didn't suit me because I got sick three times within six months, lost around 10 kg of weight, and faced work-related burnout. I was only able to socialize on weekends. Basically, it didn't work out in my favor. The day before I was leaving Bangalore, someone told me, "Everyone is happy after leaving Bangalore."

## Restart

My new office is in Pune. Here, work was much more process-oriented and less stressful. I'm also trying to figure out and have fun in ways that worked for me in Bangalore though this didn't work out here. Then I learned to enjoy Pune in its own way: having fun with colleagues, playing sports, going to movies, and walking around the streets. I'm just more living outside of my mind.

After a few months, I rented a home. At this moment, the novelty wears off for a new office, city, and people. **Now, I started living more patiently without rushing**, such as having proper breakfast, playing sports, reading books for longer, enjoying different forms of art and movies, and writing more than just journaling.

But I'm still noticing that I'm getting disappointed by the work and people around me. **Even though everything is going well, still everything feels misaligned**.

At this moment, I was reading a lot and learned the concept of complex identity: that you are more than any label; we are all these things simultaneously, so no single label describes me fully. This involves **allowing myself to have space to hold these multitudes**. Basically, you are more than your work, hobbies, gender roles, religion, or relationships. So, when one aspect of life doesn't go well, it's not the end of the world, and you may have other parts of your life which help you to be grounded and motivated. Practicing this is a lot more messier than it appears.

## Recalibration

The reason I moved out earlier was that I became comfortable in my own comfort zone. But now, after two years, I've built the same comfort zone and reached the same stagnation from the past few months. I feel the experiment is not finished yet. I realized there are still many things I care about and that I can try more. But this time, I'm going to try new things by moving back home, since I have gained more clarity, and now I don't want to put more effort into getting what I already have. I'll still be traveling for work, and **I'll be trying, exploring, but this time with more trust, safety, and confidence.**

In this process of moving out, what I gained was a bit more of myself. Also, I'm just grateful for the kind and loving people I met in this process. I really wish that certain people would have stayed longer in my life.

Crucially, many paradigm shifts happened in my thinking and approach because I was simultaneously living and reflecting, and great books allowed me to quickly course-correct my life's flow.
